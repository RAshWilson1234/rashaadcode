The response lifecycle

Six phases. One coordinated response.

Real incidents can move between phases. Keep a clear incident lead, record decisions, and follow local policy.

01 · Before

Prepare

Know the reporting channel, escalation contacts, roles, and approved response plan before an incident happens.

Ask: Who is the incident lead?
02 · Detect

Identify

Confirm what is known, when it began, what may be affected, and what remains uncertain.

Ask: What evidence supports this?
03 · Limit

Contain

Work with authorized responders to limit further impact while preserving evidence and essential operations.

Ask: What action is approved?
04 · Remove

Eradicate

Address the confirmed cause with the responsible technical team. Verify changes safely.

Ask: Has the cause been addressed?
05 · Restore

Recover

Restore services through the organization’s process, watch for recurrence, and communicate status.

Ask: What must be checked before reopening?
06 · Improve

Learn

Review the timeline and response, document lessons, and assign follow-up improvements.

Ask: What will we change next time?

Protect people and information

Do not paste secrets or real incident details into this demo. Use approved internal tools for sensitive information.

  • Never share passwords, tokens, or private records in a class exercise.
  • Do not speculate publicly about cause or attribution.
  • If safety or critical services are at risk, use emergency procedures immediately.

Classroom activities

Practice the decisions, not the panic.

Suggested exercises pending the specific activities in the lecture slides.

ACTIVITY 01 · 10 MIN

Fact or assumption?

Sort statements from a fictional alert into observed fact, assumption, and unknown. Name one safe way to verify each unknown.

ACTIVITY 02 · 15 MIN

Choose a containment path

Map who should be notified, what information responders need, and which decisions require authorization. Explain trade-offs.

ACTIVITY 03 · 10 MIN

After-action review

Review a sample timeline. Identify one thing that worked, one gap, and one specific improvement with an owner and due date.
